Code TT for Salaried Non-Exempt and Day-Rate Employees

Salaried non-exempt and day-rate workers still have an hourly regular rate. You just have to work it out first.

For a salaried non-exempt employee, divide the weekly salary by the hours it's meant to cover. For a day-rate worker, divide total day-rate pay by total hours worked. Then qualified overtime is ½ × that rate × hours over 40.

Salaried, non-exempt

A salary doesn't make someone exempt. Exempt status depends on their duties and pay level, and that is a separate legal question. For a non-exempt employee paid a salary for a fixed number of hours, the regular rate is the weekly salary divided by those hours (29 CFR 778.113).

Day rate

For a flat amount per day, the regular rate is total day-rate pay divided by total hours worked that week (29 CFR 778.112).

StepCalculationAmount
Day-rate pay5 days × $200$1,000.00
Regular rate$1,000 ÷ 50 hours$20.00
Overtime hours50 − 4010
Qualified overtime (code TT)½ × $20.00 × 10$100.00

In the calculator, enter 50 hours at a rate of $0 and put $1,000 in the bonus / other pay field. Piece-rate pay works the same way.

Salaries meant to cover whatever hours are worked (the "fluctuating workweek" method) follow different rules and aren't handled by these calculators.

Questions

Does paying a salary make an employee exempt?

No. Exempt status depends on the job's duties and the salary level. A non-exempt salaried employee is still owed overtime, and the federal premium is reported in code TT.

How do I enter a day-rate worker in the calculator?

Enter the hours worked at a rate of 0, and put the week's total day-rate pay in the bonus / other pay field. The calculator divides it by the hours to get the regular rate.
