How the codes work on the W-2

  • Box 14b holds up to two codes. If someone earned tips in two jobs, like bartending and serving, enter both. With three or more, the IRS lets you enter any two, but if any tips came from a job not on the list, one of the two must be 000.
  • Enter 000 when the job isn't on the list. Only when 000 is the sole code are the employee's tips treated as not qualifying.
  • The codes go with the tips in box 12, code TP. See how to report tips on the 2026 W-2.

What changed in the final list

The final regulations published in April 2026 kept every occupation from the 2025 proposal and added three: 509 Visual Artists, 510 Floral Designers and 810 Gas Pump Attendants. They also renamed four: 103 is now Food and Beverage Servers, Nonrestaurant; 506 is Pet and Show Animal Caretakers; 606 is Eyebrow and Eyelash Technicians; and 704 is Tour Guides (summary of the final rule; IRS announcement).

A listed job title isn't the whole test. The tips must also be voluntary, and the duties must match the occupation. A restaurant manager's tip for handling a complaint doesn't count, for example. The employee is primarily responsible for checking that their occupation is on the list.

Questions

What goes in W-2 box 14b?

The employee's Treasury Tipped Occupation Code: the three-digit code for the job in which they received tips. Up to two codes can be entered, and 000 means the job isn't on the Treasury list.

What is the code for servers and bartenders?

Wait staff are 102 and bartenders are 101. Food and beverage servers outside restaurants are 103, and bartender helpers and dining room attendants are 104.

What if an employee worked two tipped jobs?

Enter both codes in box 14b. If they had tips in three or more jobs, the IRS lets you enter any two. If any of the tips came from a job not on the list, one of the two must be 000.
